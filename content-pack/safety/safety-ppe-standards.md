# Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) Standards

## Introduction

Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) is the last line of defense against workplace injuries. It does not eliminate the hazard itself — it reduces the risk of injury when other controls (elimination, substitution, engineering) are not fully effective. PPE must be worn correctly, maintained properly, and replaced when damaged.

## PPE Hierarchy

PPE is the lowest level in the hierarchy of hazard controls:
1. **Elimination** — remove the hazard entirely (e.g. eliminate a trip hazard by rerouting a cable through the ceiling)
2. **Substitution** — replace the hazard with something less dangerous (e.g. use a less toxic cleaning solvent)
3. **Engineering controls** — isolate people from the hazard (e.g. install machine guards, ventilation systems)
4. **Administrative controls** — change how people work (e.g. rotate jobs, post warning signs, train employees)
5. **PPE** — protect the individual with equipment (e.g. gloves, goggles, helmets)

Always try to control hazards at the highest possible level. PPE should supplement, not replace, other controls.

## Categories of PPE

### Head Protection
Safety helmets (hard hats) are required in:
- Construction and renovation zones
- Warehouses with overhead storage or crane operations
- Any area marked with a "Hard Hat Required" sign

Inspect the helmet shell for cracks, dents, or damage before each use. The suspension system (the harness inside) should be replaced every 12 months regardless of visible damage, as the webbing degrades over time. The entire helmet should be replaced every 5 years from the date of first use, or immediately after any impact.

### Eye and Face Protection
Safety glasses with side shields are the minimum requirement in areas where flying particles, chemical splashes, or harmful light radiation are present. Specific requirements:
- **Chemical handling:** Chemical splash goggles (indirectly vented) plus a face shield for large-volume transfers
- **Welding operations:** Welding helmets with the correct shade lens (shade 10 for general arc welding, shade 14 for heavy arc welding)
- **Laser work:** Laser safety glasses rated for the specific wavelength and power of the laser in use

Replace scratched or pitted lenses immediately — impaired vision through damaged lenses is itself a hazard.

### Hand Protection
Gloves must be selected based on the specific hazard:
- **Cut-resistant gloves** (ANSI A4 or higher) for handling sheet metal, glass, or sharp tools
- **Chemical-resistant gloves** (nitrile for oils and solvents, butyl for ketones, neoprene for acids) — check the glove's chemical compatibility chart before selection
- **Insulated gloves** for electrical work — rated for the voltage being handled, tested every 6 months

Never use damaged or torn gloves. A torn glove provides a false sense of security while offering no protection at the tear site.

### Foot Protection
Safety footwear must meet the minimum standard for the work environment:
- **Steel-toe boots** (impact rating I/75, compression rating C/75) in warehouses, construction, and material handling areas
- **Metatarsal guards** in areas with risk of heavy objects falling on the foot
- **Static-dissipative footwear** in electronics manufacturing or flammable-material handling areas
- **Rubber boots with steel toe** in wet environments or chemical-handling areas

Safety footwear should be replaced when the toe cap is exposed, the sole is worn smooth, or the structural integrity is compromised.

## PPE Training and Compliance

Every employee issued PPE must receive training covering:
1. What PPE is required for their tasks
2. How to wear, adjust, and remove it properly
3. Limitations of the equipment
4. Inspection and maintenance procedures
5. Useful life and replacement criteria

Supervisors are responsible for enforcing PPE compliance in their areas. Any employee found not wearing required PPE will be: (1) verbally reminded on first occurrence, (2) given a written warning on the second, (3) subject to disciplinary action on the third.

## Maintenance and Storage

PPE must be cleaned and stored properly to maintain its protective qualities:
- Store in a clean, dry area away from direct sunlight and heat
- Do not store gloves inside-out — this causes the inner lining to degrade
- Replace elastic bands and straps when they lose tension
- Never share PPE that is molded to an individual (e.g. respirators, custom-fitted hearing protection) without sanitization between uses
