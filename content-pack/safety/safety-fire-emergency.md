# Fire Safety and Emergency Evacuation Procedures

## Overview

This module covers the fundamental fire safety protocols and emergency evacuation procedures that all employees must follow in the workplace. Understanding these procedures is critical for ensuring personal safety and the safety of colleagues during an emergency.

## Fire Prevention

The best response to fire is prevention. Employees must be aware of common fire hazards in the workplace:

- **Electrical equipment:** Overloaded power strips, frayed cords, and unattended heaters are the leading cause of office fires. Inspect your workstation daily for damaged cables. Report any issues to Facilities immediately.
- **Flammable materials:** Cleaning supplies, paper stock, and aerosol cans must be stored in designated areas away from heat sources. Never store flammable materials near electrical panels or break rooms.
- **Kitchen and break rooms:** Microwaves, toasters, and coffee machines must be turned off after use. Never leave cooking food unattended. Clean grease buildup from microwave ovens weekly.

## Fire Classification and Extinguisher Use

Fires are classified by the type of fuel involved, and each class requires a specific type of extinguisher:

- **Class A:** Ordinary combustibles (paper, wood, cloth, plastic). Use water or foam extinguishers.
- **Class B:** Flammable liquids (oil, gasoline, paint). Use CO2 or dry chemical extinguishers. Never use water on a Class B fire — it will spread the burning liquid.
- **Class C:** Electrical equipment. Use CO2 or clean agent extinguishers. Never use water — it conducts electricity and can cause electrocution.
- **Class K:** Kitchen oils and fats. Use wet chemical extinguishers specifically rated for Class K.

The PASS method for using a fire extinguisher: **P**ull the pin, **A**im at the base of the fire, **S**queeze the lever, **S**weep from side to side.

## Evacuation Procedures

When the fire alarm sounds:

1. **Stop all work immediately.** Do not attempt to collect personal belongings. Do not return to your desk for bags, phones, or laptops.
2. **Proceed to the nearest exit.** Follow the green exit signs. Do not use elevators under any circumstances — use stairwells only.
3. **Assist others if safe to do so.** Help colleagues with mobility issues, but do not put yourself at risk. If someone refuses to leave, note their location and inform the fire warden immediately.
4. **Proceed to the assembly point.** The primary assembly point for this building is the parking lot on the north side. The secondary assembly point (used if the primary is unsafe) is the park across the street.
5. **Remain at the assembly point until the fire warden gives the all-clear.** Do not re-enter the building under any circumstances until told to do so by the fire warden or emergency services.

## Fire Wardens

Each floor has designated fire wardens identified by orange high-visibility vests. Fire wardens are responsible for:
- Sweeping their assigned floor to ensure all employees have evacuated
- Assisting mobility-impaired individuals to safe refuges
- Reporting to the chief fire warden at the assembly point with a headcount
- Coordinating with emergency services upon arrival

If you do not know who your floor's fire warden is, ask your manager or check the safety notice board near the main elevator lobby.
