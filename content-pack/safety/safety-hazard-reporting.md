# Workplace Hazard Identification and Reporting

## Purpose

Every employee is responsible for identifying and reporting hazards in the workplace. A hazard is any condition, practice, or behavior that could cause injury, illness, or property damage. Early identification and reporting prevents accidents before they occur.

## Types of Workplace Hazards

### Physical Hazards
These are environmental factors that can harm the body without necessarily touching it:
- Wet or uneven flooring — the most common cause of workplace slips, trips, and falls
- Poor lighting in stairwells or storage areas
- Excessive noise in manufacturing or loading areas (sustained exposure above 85 dB requires hearing protection)
- Extreme temperatures in warehouses or cold-storage facilities

### Chemical Hazards
Exposure to harmful substances through inhalation, skin contact, or ingestion:
- Cleaning agents and solvents used by janitorial staff
- Toner and ink in print rooms
- Pesticides used in landscaping areas
- Any substance with a Safety Data Sheet (SDS) — SDS sheets are stored in the blue binder in each department's supply closet

### Ergonomic Hazards
Repetitive strain and poor posture injuries that develop over time:
- Incorrect monitor height causing neck strain
- Unsupportive seating leading to lower back pain
- Repetitive typing without wrist support
- Prolonged sitting without breaks — employees should stand and stretch for 2 minutes every 30 minutes

### Psychosocial Hazards
Workplace stress, harassment, and fatigue:
- Excessive workload or unrealistic deadlines
- Workplace bullying or harassment — report to HR or use the anonymous hotline at ext. 5500
- Shift work and sleep deprivation affecting judgment and reaction time

## How to Report a Hazard

### Immediate Danger
If a hazard poses an immediate threat to safety (e.g. a chemical spill, exposed wiring, a blocked fire exit), take these steps in order:
1. Ensure your own safety first — do not approach the hazard if it puts you at risk
2. Warn others in the vicinity verbally and, if possible, cordon off the area
3. Call Facilities at ext. 4400 during business hours, or Security at ext. 9900 after hours
4. Submit a formal hazard report within 24 hours

### Non-Immediate Hazards
For hazards that do not pose an immediate threat (e.g. a flickering light, a wobbly chair, a worn carpet edge):
1. Submit a hazard report through the internal portal under Safety > Report a Hazard
2. Include: location, description, photos if possible, and your assessment of severity (low/medium/high)
3. Facilities will acknowledge the report within 2 business days and provide a resolution timeline

## The Hazard Report Form

Every hazard report must include:
- **Date and time of observation**
- **Location** (building, floor, room or area description)
- **Hazard type** (physical, chemical, ergonomic, psychosocial)
- **Description** — what you observed, in factual terms
- **Severity** — your assessment: Low (minor injury possible), Medium (serious injury possible), High (life-threatening or could cause permanent harm)
- **People at risk** — who is exposed to this hazard (e.g. "all employees on floor 3", "janitorial staff only")
- **Suggested control** — if you have a recommendation, include it; if not, leave blank

## Follow-Up

Once a hazard report is submitted, the responsible department (Facilities, HR, or IT depending on hazard type) will:
1. Investigate within 2 business days for medium/high severity, 5 business days for low severity
2. Implement a control measure — elimination, substitution, engineering control, administrative control, or PPE, in that order of preference
3. Close the report with a summary of the action taken

You will be notified when the report is closed. If you believe the resolution is inadequate, you can escalate to the Safety Committee by replying to the closure notification.
